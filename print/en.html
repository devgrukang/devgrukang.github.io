Part 2 · Cover letter

A standard left behind changes the site

자기소개서4 questions · Asan, Korea

1Why all eight were full-time

My name is Eunsung Kang. I have spent 17 years and 5 months in full-time work, and most of it on the floor of a plant, a warehouse or a site. I started in IT, moved into vehicle assembly at SsangYong Motor for 6 years 11 months, inspected the outgoing packaging of glass materials at Corning Precision Materials, drove and managed freight at Kyungdong Parcel, taught IT to adults, and most recently handled permits, materials and schedules for photovoltaic power plants.

The products and the job titles changed every time. What did not change was the actual work: go and see the problem, agree a standard with the people around me, write it down so it survives me, and use the numbers to find where the problem started. That is the part of the job I am good at, and it is the part I want to keep doing.

2Coding the factory floor

At Corning Precision Materials, inspectors judged the same product slightly differently, so the same item would pass with one person and be sent for a second look by another. I grouped defect types, gave them codes and summarised them in Excel so the frequent ones became visible, then wrote the inspection procedure down and put it beside the bench. Arguments over the same issue became fewer and new staff learned faster.

At SsangYong Motor I checked incoming parts with purchasing and quality staff. When a part did not meet specification the line had to stop for a decision, so I recorded photographs, quantity and time of occurrence, sent them to the supplier and confirmed the countermeasure. After 6 years 11 months on the same line I knew which defects appear at which process, and I learned that written criteria and the sense gained on the floor have to be used together or judgement drifts.

At my last employer I handled permit documents, authority coordination, material supply and contractor schedules for power plants, and I also led the build of two company websites: choosing the vendor, managing contract and schedule, preparing the content and deciding the design direction. Different work, same method — decide what has to be true, put it in a document, and follow it until it is finished.

3Few words, finished work

My strength is diligence. I was born in Pyeongtaek and never missed a perfect-attendance award or a full attendance record at school. That may sound like a small thing to say, but keeping a standard comes down to turning up at the same place every day and repeating the same procedure. I have never been absent without notice in any of the eight workplaces.

The gap I am aware of is depth in a single specialty. I have worked across production, quality, logistics, IT and site administration rather than staying in one field for 17 years. That gives me an understanding of how the departments around me actually work; what it costs me is that I have to learn the technical detail of a new field from the beginning. I read procedures and specifications carefully, ask until I understand, and write down what I am told so I do not ask twice.

4Ask once, write it down

In the first month I would learn how the site runs: who does what, where work stops, and which documents actually govern the job. Then I would take the part I am given and make it visible — a checklist, a tracking sheet or a short written procedure — so that the state of the work can be seen by anyone without asking me.

I do not need a title to start. What I want is a place where the work has a standard and someone has to keep it, and where being careful and finishing things is worth something. With 17 years and 5 months of full-time experience, I intend this next position to be a long-term commitment.
